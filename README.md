## 🦈 Shark Attack Analysis: Global Patterns and Risk Assessment (2018-2020) 
This analysis examines global shark attack incidents recorded between 2018 and 2020 to identify emerging patterns, risk factors, and geographic trends. By analyzing this three-year dataset, I investigated the circumstances surrounding shark encounters, focusing on environmental conditions, human activities, and seasonal variations during this recent period. The findings reveal a clear concentration of incidents, within three countries dominating the global landscape: the United States (particularly Florida and Hawaii), Australia, and South Africa. These nations accounted for the majority of reported attacks from 2018-2020, reflecting their combination of extensive coastlines, popular water recreation areas, and significant shark populations. While the data confirms these regions as higher-risk zones, the analysis also underscores that the absolute probability of shark encounters remains extremely low, providing important context for both public safety measures and shark conservation efforts.

## 🎯 True Risk Assessment

### ⚠️ Highest Absolute Risk:
Countries with the MOST attacks represent the greatest overall danger because they have the highest probability of ANY encounter occurring

### ⚠️ Highest Fatality Risk:
Countries with high fatality rates represent greater danger PER encounter, but may have fewer total incidents

### 💡 Correct Interpretation:
For practical safety concerns, attack FREQUENCY matters more than fatality rates when assessing which countries are 'riskiest'

## 💡 Safety Recommendations
